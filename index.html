Welcome

Five short games about the extremes of the world's countries and territories, made for teaching and training. Each game shows how a country compares with the rest, and every answer comes with the figures behind it.

Elevation

Highest points, lowest points and average elevation, measured against sea level.

Climate

Record highs and lows, average daily highs and lows, average temperature and rainfall.

Each theme has a comparison game and a guessing game. In the comparison games (higher or lower, hotter or colder and wetter or drier) you choose between two countries and keep a streak going until you get one wrong. In the guessing games you play ten rounds, name the number, and score more points the closer you are, at one of four difficulty levels.

About the data

Where the figures come from

Elevation

Highest and lowest points come from the Wikipedia page List of elevation extremes by country. Average elevations come from List of countries by average elevation.

Record temperatures

The hottest and coldest temperatures ever recorded in each country come from List of weather records on Wikipedia. Where the page lists several records for one country, such as Russia or the United States, the hottest or the coldest is used. Records at shared or disputed locations are left out of the games.

Average temperatures

Average daily highs, average daily lows and average yearly temperatures cover 1991 to 2020 and come from the World Bank Climate Change Knowledge Portal, which uses Climatic Research Unit gridded data (version 4.07). This is the one source that is not Wikipedia.

Rainfall

Rainfall comes from List of countries by average annual precipitation on Wikipedia. It is the average over the whole country, not the wettest place in it.

Units

Figures are shown in metric units by default: metres, degrees Celsius and millimetres. Every game has a Metric or Imperial switch on its start screen for feet, degrees Fahrenheit and inches. Your choice is remembered in your browser and applies to all the games.

Countries and maps

The games cover the 249 countries and territories on the ISO 3166-1 list, plus Kosovo, which has no code on it. A country with no figure in a source is left out of the questions that need that figure. Map outlines come from Natural Earth. Wikipedia figures were fetched on 29 October 2026.
